Your November focus
Back Development + Pelvic Floor Strategy
We are not replacing strength training with rehab. We pair purposeful lifting with breathing and pressure control. Exhale through effort, avoid breath-holding or bearing down, and adjust range, load or position if you notice pelvic pressure, heaviness, leaking or pain.

Lats · Mid-Back · Rear Delts · Biceps

Back session #1. Build lat width and upper-back detail with controlled reps. Progress load only when you can keep your torso steady and breathe through the hard part.

4 × 6–8
Pull elbows down toward your sides. Keep chest tall without leaning back. Why: strong vertical pull for back width.

Reverse Cable Fly

Bayesian Cable Curl
3 × 8–10
Keep upper arm slightly behind torso; control the stretch. Why: supports the pulling work with direct biceps training.
Dead Bug Heel Reach + Exhale
3 × 6–8 each
Exhale as heel reaches away; shorten range if ribs lift or breathing changes. Why: practices trunk/pelvic-floor coordination without bearing down.

Tuesday · Solo
Glutes · Strength + Shape
Hip Extension · Single Leg · Glute Med · Core
▼
Keep glute work challenging, but do not turn every rep into a maximal brace. Exhale through the effort and let the hips do the work.
Smith Machine Glute Bridge
5 × 6–8
Set the Smith bar across the hips with a pad. Feet about hip-width, shins near vertical at the top. Exhale, drive through the whole foot and finish with ribs stacked—no low-back arch. Why: heavy, stable hip extension with direct glute emphasis.
Dumbbell Step-Up
3 × 6–8 each
Choose a box height you can control without pushing off the trailing foot. Why: builds single-leg glute strength and pelvic control.
Heavy Landmine Sumo Squat → Wall Sit
3 rounds · 8–10 squats + 30–45 sec wall sit
Perform 8–10 controlled, heavy landmine sumo squats, then move directly to a wall sit for 30–45 seconds. Rest after the wall sit. Keep knees tracking with toes and use a depth you can control. Why: combines loaded adductor/glute work with a simple quad finisher.
Seated Hip Abduction
3 × 10–12
Open knees smoothly, pause and return with control. Why: strengthens lateral hip muscles.
Curtsy Lunge
3 × 10 each side
Step one foot diagonally behind the other, keeping the front foot planted and front knee tracking in line with toes. Use a comfortable crossover distance; keep hips mostly square and torso controlled. Hold support if balance is limiting. Why: adds a controlled single-leg glute and hip challenge.
Single-Leg Elevated Bridge
3 × 15 each side
Lie on your back with one heel elevated on a low bench or step and the other leg lifted. Exhale, drive through the elevated heel and lift hips without arching the low back. Keep pelvis level; reduce height if hamstrings cramp.

This is the main coached back session. Compare the Smith inverted row with October’s bar height and reps. When all prescribed reps are controlled, make the angle slightly harder or reduce assistance. No grinding or bearing down.
Smith Machine Assisted Inverted Row
5 × 6–8
Record bar height and reps so we can compare week to week. Pull chest toward bar, keep body long and lower under control. Why: measurable back-thickness progression.
Smith bar progression — each set: Start at the October bar height (or the height that allows clean reps). After each set of 6–8, lower the bar by ONE setting only if every rep was controlled, chest reached the bar, hips stayed lifted, and breathing stayed steady. If reps get sloppy, you cannot reach the target, or you feel pelvic pressure/heaviness/leaking/pain, keep the same height or raise it one setting. Record the bar setting for all 5 sets. The goal is better strength and control—not forcing the bar lower.

Fuel is part of the program
You cannot build muscle on empty

You are training with heavier loads now. Your body needs enough total food, protein, carbohydrates and fats to support strength, recovery and muscle growth. Eating less is not the goal. Under-fueling can make it harder to add weight, recover between sessions and see the progress we are working for.

Track consistently, not perfectly. Log meals, snacks, drinks and portions for the next 7 days, including training and rest days. The point is to see whether you are eating enough—not to judge the numbers or chase restriction. Bring the log to Christin so we can make informed adjustments.

If tracking feels stressful or triggers restrictive thoughts, tell Christin and we will use a different check-in method.
